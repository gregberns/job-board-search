## Patterns Observed

### What the user actually wants (distilled from positive signals)

1. **Large, complex industries with high value** -- Construction (PermitFlow), industrial operations (Kallikor, Gecko), supply chain (Vorto, Terminal). The user lights up when the industry is big, messy, and ripe for transformation.

2. **Business systems that drive change, not just efficiency** -- Repeatedly rejected "efficiency gains only" companies (all legal, Overstory). The user wants to build systems that fundamentally change how work gets done, not just make existing workflows 10% faster.

3. **Feedback loops and leverage** -- Explicitly called out wanting feedback loops (Overstory pass) and control over leverage. Prefers companies where the software IS the product and directly drives outcomes, not one tool in a large ecosystem.

4. **AI applied to the physical world** -- Positive signals on Augury, Gecko, Terminal, Kallikor, SewerAI. The intersection of AI + physical operations is clearly exciting.

5. **FP-adjacent stacks** -- Haskell (Hasura), Rust (Foxglove, Nominal), Elixir (V7, Duffel), OCaml (Ahrefs, Imandra) all got positive stack mentions. The user values teams that choose these languages.

6. **Remote or location-flexible** -- Multiple interesting companies were blocked solely by on-site requirements. Remote is not a nice-to-have; it's a hard filter.

### What we thought they wanted vs. reality

- **Legal tech was a miss.** We included 3 legal companies and all were passed. The user sees legal as "efficiency gains only" -- making lawyers work less isn't transformative enough.

- **Healthcare was weaker than expected.** Domain is compelling but the user doesn't feel connected to the impact. Two healthcare companies were blocked by location anyway.

- **Climate/energy was mixed.** Overstory and Gridware both passed despite being "honorable work." The issue isn't the mission -- it's that these feel like utility services with incremental impact and limited leverage.

- **Consumer technology is a hard no.** Standard AI and Sense were quickly dismissed. This should be a filter in future searches.

- **Infrastructure-for-infrastructure is a no.** Modal (serverless compute) was too meta -- building infra for others to build on. The user wants to be closer to the end problem.

- **BI/analytics is a no.** Metabase was passed because the user doesn't want to go back to that space. Prior experience, not interested in repeating.

- **Defense is questionable.** Hadrian was a soft pass. Not a hard no but not a draw.

### Surprises

- **PermitFlow was the strongest positive.** Construction permitting is unsexy but the user immediately recognized the complexity, value, and opportunity. This tells us: look for regulated, complex, high-value industries that most engineers wouldn't think of.

- **Kallikor got strong interest with almost no information.** Digital twin simulations of real operations hit a nerve. The Cognite connection is clear -- this is the archetype.

- **Imandra was the most enthusiastic response ("I'd love to work here") but is blocked** by prior non-response. This is worth revisiting -- perhaps a warm intro or different approach.

- **On-site requirements blocked 5+ otherwise-interesting companies.** Location is the single biggest practical filter. Future searches should lead with remote-first filtering.

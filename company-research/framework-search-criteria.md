# Framework Search Criteria

Translating the four-filter flywheel into searchable company signals.

---

## The Flywheel Recap

```
Value -> Lever -> Compound -> More Value (not linear -- exponential)
```

1. **Dead inside?** Gut check. The company does something that matters in the world.
2. **Hard, important problem?** Not convenience, not efficiency, not incremental. The software enables what wasn't possible before.
3. **Software/AI IS the lever?** Remove it and value collapses -- not just gets less convenient.
4. **Outsized impact?** Improvements compound. More data -> better models -> better outcomes -> more data. Features build on each other.

---

## What Passes All Four Filters

Companies that pass tend to share a pattern: **AI applied to hard physical-world or operational problems where the AI creates a feedback loop that compounds**.

### Signals that a company passes

- The core product IS the AI/software, not a wrapper around manual processes
- Customers literally cannot do what they do without this product (not "do it faster")
- Every deployment generates data that makes the product better for everyone
- A 5% improvement in the model translates to measurable real-world outcomes (safety, yield, throughput, cost)
- The problem space has high variance and complexity that humans can't optimize alone
- Domain expertise is encoded in the models, not just in a knowledge base

### Signals that a company fails

- "We use AI to automate X" where X is paperwork, compliance, or communication
- The AI is a feature, not the product (chatbot bolted onto a traditional business)
- Value is linear: add a feature, slightly better experience, no flywheel
- Could replace the software with more humans and get 80% of the value
- The business model is "sell to other software companies" (devtools/infra)
- Domain is fundamentally about cost reduction, not capability expansion

## Industries with the Flywheel

Industries where software-as-lever with compounding tends to work:

### Tier 1: Strong flywheel signal

- **Industrial operations / manufacturing** -- sensor data + AI = predictive models that compound across every facility. Cognite pattern: every data source and AI improvement compounds across every customer.
- **Autonomous construction/earthmoving** -- every job site generates training data. Better models = safer, faster, more precise operations. Labor shortage makes AI the only path, not an optimization.
- **Precision agriculture** -- crop data + weather + soil = yield predictions that improve every season. Each field is training data for the next. AI literally replaces impossible manual observation.
- **Mining operations** -- autonomous haulage, 3D mapping of underground spaces. Safety-critical, data-rich, compounding. Humans can't go where the robots go.
- **Energy grid optimization** -- real-time balancing of supply/demand with renewables. Every decision improves the model. Linear thinking (human dispatchers) literally cannot handle the complexity.
- **Materials science/discovery** -- AI screens billions of compositions. Each experiment refines the models. Compress millennia of trial-and-error into months.

### Tier 2: Good signal, verify the lever

- **Logistics/supply chain (where AI IS the brain)** -- demand prediction, autonomous routing. Must verify: is the AI the lever, or is it just tracking/surveillance?
- **Clinical trials operations** -- real-time data consolidation, protocol optimization. Verify: is the AI making decisions, or just dashboarding?
- **Environmental monitoring** -- satellite/sensor data for water, forests, emissions. Verify: does the data compound, or is it static measurement?
- **Construction design/QC** -- AI reviewing drawings against building codes. Verify: does accuracy compound with more projects?

### Tier 3: Proceed with caution

- **Fleet management** -- often surveillance, not optimization. Only passes if AI is genuinely the decision-maker.
- **Drug discovery** -- fascinating science, but feedback loops are very slow (years for clinical validation).
- **Digital twins** -- technically interesting but impact often indirect. Passes only when the twin IS the operational decision-maker.

## What Makes a Company "AI-First" (in the Way That Matters)

Not "we use AI" but "AI IS the lever":

| AI-first (passes) | AI-added (fails) |
|---|---|
| Remove AI and the product doesn't exist | Remove AI and you have a slightly worse SaaS tool |
| AI model quality IS the competitive moat | Features and integrations are the moat |
| Every customer's data improves the model | Data is siloed per customer |
| AI makes decisions humans cannot | AI makes suggestions humans could figure out |
| 10x improvement comes from better models | 10x improvement comes from more features |
| Core team is ML/robotics engineers | Core team is product/sales with an ML team bolted on |

### Questions to ask about any "AI company"

1. What happens if you remove the AI? Does the value collapse, or just degrade?
2. Does deployment data flow back into model improvement?
3. Is the AI replacing human judgment that is genuinely insufficient (not just expensive)?
4. Does a 5% model improvement create a measurable business outcome?
5. Is the founding team ML/domain-native, or did they bolt on AI later?

## "Business Value Impact" -- What Industries Have Software-as-Lever with Compounding

The highest-leverage industries share characteristics:

- **High consequence per decision** -- a wrong call costs lives, millions, or months
- **High variance in inputs** -- no two fields/mines/sites/grids are the same
- **Data-rich but insight-poor** -- sensors everywhere, but no one can process it all
- **Labor-constrained** -- not enough skilled humans, or too dangerous for humans
- **Physical world** -- atoms, not bits. The real world doesn't have an undo button.

These industries create natural flywheels because:
- Each deployment generates unique data
- The AI learns from conditions no human could have anticipated
- Better AI = safer/faster/cheaper operations = more deployments = more data
- Competitors can't replicate the data moat

## Search Strategy

### Query patterns that find flywheel companies

- "[domain] AI autonomous startup Series A B C funding 2025 2026"
- "AI applied to [physical domain] compounding data flywheel"
- "[domain] startup where software IS the product"
- "YC companies AI [domain] operations physical world"
- Bessemer/a16z/Khosla portfolio companies in physical AI

### Domains to search systematically

1. Construction (beyond permitting -- earthmoving, design QC, site monitoring)
2. Agriculture (precision farming, autonomous harvesting, crop intelligence)
3. Mining (autonomous haulage, underground mapping, predictive maintenance)
4. Energy (grid balancing, renewable integration, demand optimization)
5. Materials science (AI-driven discovery, autonomous labs)
6. Industrial maintenance (predictive, not just reactive)
7. Water/environmental (real-time monitoring with compounding models)
8. Clinical trials (operational intelligence, not just drug discovery)
9. Bulk logistics (where AI IS the brain, not surveillance)

### Red flags in search results

- Company website says "platform" more than it explains what the AI does
- Use cases are all about "saving time" or "reducing costs"
- No mention of data flywheel or model improvement
- Team page is all business people, few ML/robotics engineers
- Customer testimonials are about "ease of use" not "capability we didn't have before"

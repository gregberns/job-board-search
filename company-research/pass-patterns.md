# Pass Patterns -- Filter Criteria for Future Searches
*Distilled from user feedback on 35 companies, 2026-04-13.*

Use these patterns to pre-filter future company research batches. Each pattern
includes the evidence (which companies triggered it) and the filter rule.

---

## Hard Passes (auto-filter in future)

### 1. Legal industry
**Evidence:** Darrow, Orbital, Norm AI, Ivo -- all passed.
**User signal:** "Legal isn't going to tick my boxes." "The real world impact -- make lawyers work less." "The impact isn't very interesting -- other than efficiency."
**Filter:** Skip legal tech companies entirely. The domain produces efficiency gains, not transformation.

### 2. Consumer technology
**Evidence:** Standard AI, Sense -- both passed immediately.
**User signal:** "I don't think this is the right path. Its very consumer focused." "Not interested in consumer technology."
**Filter:** Skip B2C companies. Include only B2B or enterprise-facing businesses.

### 3. Pure infrastructure / infra-for-infra
**Evidence:** Modal -- passed.
**User signal:** "This is a lot of infrastructure work - not my cup of tea."
**Filter:** Skip companies whose product is developer infrastructure or compute platform. User wants to be closer to the end problem being solved.

### 4. BI / analytics products
**Evidence:** Metabase -- passed.
**User signal:** "Not sure I want to go back into BI product."
**Filter:** Skip BI, dashboarding, and analytics tool companies. User has prior experience here and doesn't want to repeat.

---

## Soft Passes (flag but don't auto-filter)

### 5. Defense / military
**Evidence:** Hadrian -- soft pass.
**User signal:** "Not sure defense is the right area for me."
**Filter:** Flag defense companies but don't auto-filter. Could be overridden by exceptional fit.

### 6. Healthcare -- uncertain
**Evidence:** Ambience, Abridge, C the Signs -- mixed but no strong positives.
**User signal:** "Not sure about healthcare overall." "Brings efficiencies to healthcare." (lukewarm)
**Filter:** Include healthcare only if the AI application is genuinely transformative (not just clinical documentation efficiency). The domain is compelling but the user doesn't feel connected to the impact.

### 7. Climate/energy as utility service
**Evidence:** Overstory, Gridware -- both passed despite being "honorable work."
**User signal:** "I'm not sure it has the feedback loop I'm looking for. Its one piece in a very large puzzle. I'd like to control more of the leverage." "Impact seems incremental."
**Filter:** Climate/energy companies pass only if the software IS the core product with direct feedback loops. Skip if the company is one monitoring layer in a large utility ecosystem.

---

## Blockers (interested but blocked)

### 8. On-site requirement (non-negotiable unless exceptional)
**Evidence:** Gecko Robotics (east coast), Terminal Industries (Texas), Vorto (Denver), Ambience (California hybrid), Abridge (Bay Area), Deeptune (NYC).
**User signal:** Every one of these companies had positive feedback followed by a location blocker.
**Filter:** Lead with remote-first or remote-OK filtering. On-site is a hard blocker. Do not spend research time on companies that require on-site unless the opportunity is truly exceptional.

### 9. Engineering team outside US
**Evidence:** Darrow (Israel), Orbital (London).
**User signal:** "It also looks like the engineering is all in Israel." "Also looks like Engineering is out of London."
**Filter:** Verify engineering team location. Non-US engineering hubs (especially Israel, UK) are a blocker for remote US-based work.

### 10. Company not hiring / previously rejected
**Evidence:** Ahrefs (not hiring), Imandra (applied, no response).
**User signal:** "They aren't hiring." "I've applied and they haven't ever responded back."
**Filter:** Verify active hiring before deep research. For previously-rejected companies, only include if there's a warm intro path or the situation has changed.

---

## Positive Signals (what TO look for)

### A. Large, complex, regulated industries
**Evidence:** PermitFlow (strongest positive), Kallikor, Gecko Robotics, Terminal Industries.
**User signal:** "There is a lot of opportunity for automation in construction, theres a ton of value, its a large industry, and very complex and involved."
**Search for:** Construction, logistics, manufacturing, supply chain, industrial operations, permitting, compliance in physical industries.

### B. Digital twins / simulation of real operations
**Evidence:** Kallikor (strong positive), Nominal (interested), Cognite mentioned as archetype.
**User signal:** "From first glance this looks really cool." (Kallikor) -- user connected immediately to the Cognite archetype.
**Search for:** Digital twin companies, operational simulation, warehouse/factory/network modeling.

### C. AI applied to physical world operations
**Evidence:** Augury, Gecko, Terminal, SewerAI, Kallikor -- all got positive or interested responses.
**User signal:** "Bring AI to the physical world." "Challenging problem bringing AI into the real world."
**Search for:** Industrial IoT + AI, physical operations optimization, predictive maintenance (if platform-focused).

### D. Software IS the product (not a feature)
**Evidence:** Passes on Overstory, Gridware (monitoring layers). Positive on PermitFlow, Kallikor (software drives the outcome).
**User signal:** Wants "feedback loops" and "control over leverage."
**Search for:** Companies where the software platform directly drives business outcomes, not companies where software is a monitoring/alerting layer on top of physical processes.

### E. FP-adjacent tech stacks
**Evidence:** Positive mentions of Haskell (Hasura), Rust (Foxglove, Nominal), Elixir (V7, Duffel), OCaml (Ahrefs, Imandra).
**User signal:** Stack quality is a draw but not sufficient alone (Ahrefs has great stack but passed on domain).
**Search for:** Companies using Haskell, Rust, Elixir, OCaml, Scala, or teams that value PL research. Stack alone won't save a bad domain fit, but it's a strong tiebreaker.

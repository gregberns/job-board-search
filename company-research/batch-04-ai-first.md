## 1. Carbon Robotics

**What they do:** AI-powered laser weeding for agriculture. Computer vision identifies crops vs weeds in real-time across 42 cameras, then kills weeds with precision lasers. Eliminates herbicides entirely.

**Why it passes all four filters:**
1. Dead inside? No -- eliminating herbicides from farming is consequential
2. Hard important problem? Yes -- 200,000 weeds/hour, real-time plant identification at speed
3. AI is the lever? Yes -- the Large Plant Model trained on 150M plants IS the product. Remove AI and you have an expensive tractor with lasers that can't aim.
4. Compounds? Yes -- every field in every country adds training data. LPM improves across all deployments. More crops identified = more markets = more data.

**Stage:** Series D ($177M total, $70M Series D)
**Team size:** ~150
**Location:** Seattle, WA (hybrid/remote options)
**Tech stack:** Computer vision, deep learning, embedded systems, cloud infrastructure, real-time GPU compute

## 5. Field AI

**What they do:** Universal autonomy brain for robots in unstructured environments. Field Foundation Models (FFMs) enable any robot (quadrupeds, humanoids, wheeled, vehicles) to navigate and operate in unmapped, GPS-denied environments without human intervention. Spun out of NASA JPL / DARPA challenges.

**Why it passes all four filters:**
1. Dead inside? No -- teaching robots to navigate the real world like explorers. Construction, mining, disaster response.
2. Hard important problem? Yes -- unstructured environments (mines, construction sites, disaster zones) are the hardest problem in robotics. No GPS, no maps, no structure.
3. AI is the lever? Yes -- the FFMs ARE the product. Hardware-agnostic -- the intelligence works across any robot form factor.
4. Compounds? Yes -- explicit data flywheel. "The first company to deploy intelligent robots at scale kicks off a compounding advantage in capturing real-world edge cases."
